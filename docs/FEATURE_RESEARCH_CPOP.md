# C-pop Fan-Platform Engagement Mechanics — Research Addendum

## 1. Weibo 超话 (Super Topics)

### Core mechanics
- **Super Topic = a Reddit-style sub-community** pinned to a celebrity, drama, game, or CP (ship). It sits outside the main Weibo feed, so fans get a semi-private clubhouse with its own posting norms, sticky posts (置顶帖), and a curated "essentials" tab (精花).
- **Governance is fan-run**: each Super Topic has up to **3 主持人 (hosts) + 10 副主持人 (sub-hosts)** who control pinning, featuring, and moderation. This is the single most under-copied part — the platform delegates curation labor to the most invested fans and pays them in status.
- **签到 (daily check-in)**: one tap per day per Super Topic. It is the atomic unit of the whole system.
- **Points → personal level**: check-ins, posting, commenting, reposting, and liking all accrue 积分 that raise your level inside that Super Topic. Higher levels unlock privileges within the community.
- **名人堂 (Hall of Fame)** tab: lists hosts, top contributors, and — critically — **the "biggest fan," determined by longest consecutive check-in streak**. Identity is awarded for consistency, not spend.

### What drives DAILY return
The streak. Because top-fan status is defined by *consecutive* days, a single missed day is not a small loss — it resets a public, permanent identity claim. This is the strongest daily-return primitive in the entire Chinese fan stack, and it costs the user nothing but one tap. Secondary driver: the fandom's leaderboard position is recomputed daily, so "we drop a rank if you skip today" is a real, checkable claim organizers make to members.

### Fandom-vs-fandom competition
Super Topics are **ranked against each other by popularity within categories** (actors, TV drama, games, sports, food, photography). Rank is a function of reads, discussion volume, follower count, and check-in activity. Fan organizers publish daily check-in counts and run "check-in drives" the way political campaigns run GOTV. Ship/CP Super Topics compete as their own entities alongside individual-idol ones.

### What was banned
- **明星势力榜 (Star Power List), live since 2014, was taken offline on 6 August 2021.** It ranked celebrities by followers, activity, engagement, social influence, and "positive energy," segmented by region.
- The behaviors it drove are the explicit red lines: **"data fans" (数据粉) running multiple accounts daily**; **paid virtual gifts as ranking fuel** (one campaign reportedly spent close to 10 million yuan on virtual flowers); and **"moving home" campaigns** where a fandom collectively spent to migrate their idol into a stronger regional chart.
- CAC directive: *"Cancel all star artist lists along with all ranking lists involving celebrities… the addition of new or disguised lists of personalities, or their related products or functions is forbidden."* Note the phrase **"or disguised"** — reskinning a celebrity leaderboard does not clear the rule.
- **What survived: the Super Topic list itself.** Sixth Tone explicitly noted the Super Topics ranking continued operating after the Star Power List was killed, despite also driving intense fan competition. The distinction that saved it: it ranks **communities by activity**, not **people by popularity**, and its inputs are free actions rather than purchases.

## 2. Survival-show voting culture (iQiyi / Tencent)

### The mechanic that broke
*Youth With You 3* (iQiyi, 2021) tiered votes by spend:
- Free user: **1 vote/day**
- VIP subscriber: **2 votes/day**
- Buyer of sponsor Mengniu's milk drink: **+5 votes/day**, redeemed via a QR code **printed inside the bottle cap**

Because the code was under the cap, the milk was a byproduct. Fan groups and hired vote-farmers bulk-bought cases, scanned caps, and **poured the milk into ditches**. Video of the dumping went viral days after China's **Anti-Food-Waste Law** took effect. Beijing's municipal broadcasting regulator ordered the finale suspended; iQiyi cancelled it and apologized.

Adjacent mechanics from the same era: fan groups bought **burner phone numbers and bulk accounts** to multiply daily free votes, and paid **professional voting agencies (代投)** to scan thousands of codes per day. *Chuang 2021* top-11 fandoms collectively raised roughly **US$23.6 million** by April 2021 to fund this.

### What drives DAILY return
Hard daily vote caps with **use-it-or-lose-it expiry**. A vote not cast today is destroyed. Combined with a live public tally and elimination episodes on a weekly clock, this produced the highest-intensity daily loop in the category — and, precisely because it was monetized, the one regulators destroyed.

### Fandom-vs-fandom competition
Zero-sum by construction: a fixed number of debut slots, a visible rank ladder, and weekly eliminations. Fandoms formed alliances ("mutual vote" pacts) and ran 公开集资 (public fundraising) with published ledgers.

### What was banned
- **Beijing Municipal Radio and Television Bureau, May 2021**: competition shows may no longer run **any voting system where fans spend money to vote**.
- **NRTA 8-point plan, 2 September 2021**: **idol-selection/cultivation shows are not to be made or aired at all**; voting must be strictly controlled; **encouraging fans to buy products for an idol's ranking or votes is forbidden**; plus celebrity pay caps.
- **CAC, August 2021**: platforms may not induce minors to fund idols or participate in fan fundraising; ban on wealth-flaunting content, comment manipulation, and manufactured trending topics.

### What survived, and in what form
- **The format moved offshore** (*Chuang Asia: Thailand Edition*, *Asia Super Young*, 2023 onward) with non-monetized voting.
- **Voting survived only as a free, capped, identity-verified action** — no purchase path, no VIP multiplier, no per-account stacking.
- **Ranking migrated from people to works**: CAC allowed film/TV/music charts to remain provided they name no stars and downweight likes/comments in favor of professional evaluation.
- Demand re-routed into **livestream idol studios / 团播 (short-video group performance)**, a sector analysts size in the billions of yuan. *[unverified — search-summary figure, primary source not opened]*

## 3. bilibili

### Core mechanics
- **硬币 (coins)** — a *scarce* internal currency. Users get roughly **1 coin per day for logging in** (plus small task grants); coins cannot be freely farmed. You may give **at most 2 coins to any single video**.
- **一键三连 ("one-key triple")** — long-press the like button to fire **like + coin + favorite** simultaneously. Creators end videos by begging for it; the phrase is a cultural meme in its own right.
- **Levels Lv0–Lv6** driven by daily EXP with a **daily cap** (~65 EXP/day via login, watching, sharing, liking, coin-giving). Because the cap is per-day, level is a pure function of *how many days you showed up* — you cannot buy or binge your way up.
- **粉丝勋章 (fan medals)** — creators with **1,000+ followers** can enable a wearable badge. Levels 1–20: earned via free/cheap gifts (a free grind). Levels 21–40: require paid gold gifts / 大航海 membership, with a daily cap of 250,000 power points. Buying a 大航海 tier jumps you straight to level 21.
- **大航海 ("Great Voyage") subscription tiers**: 舰长 (Captain) ¥198 / 提督 (Admiral) ¥1,998 / 总督 (Governor) ¥19,998 — the paid loyalty ladder attached to a streamer.
- **Danmaku (弹幕)** — comments scroll across the video timeline, anchored to timestamps. Everyone watching a video, whenever they watch it, appears to watch it *together*. It converts asynchronous viewing into a synthetic live crowd.

### What drives DAILY return
Coin scarcity is the engine. Because you receive coins on a **daily drip** and they are consumed by giving, the only way to support many creators is to come back every day. The EXP daily cap does the same for the level ladder. Both are **time-gated, not money-gated** — which is exactly why they survived a regulatory environment that killed pay-to-rank.

### Fandom-vs-fandom competition
Softer than Weibo's. Competition is **creator-vs-creator** and **within-fandom status** (medal level / 大航海 tier displayed next to your danmaku, so your rank is visible every time you speak). The badge-next-to-your-name pattern turns every comment into a status broadcast.

### Regulatory exposure
bilibili's core loop was largely untouched by the 清朗 fan-circle rules because it ranks **content**, not celebrities, and the free tier is fully playable. The exposed surface is 大航海 / paid medal levels, close to the "flaunting wealth / minors spending" red line. Treat paid-tier badges as the risky half and free-grind badges as the safe half. *[Tipping-by-minors rules noted but primary source not opened.]*

## 4. NetEase Cloud Music

### Core mechanics
- **The comment section is the product.** 热评 (hot comments) — comments ranked by likes under each track, where users leave confessional micro-essays about heartbreak, memory, and loss. Users describe reading hot comments as the reason they open the app, independent of listening.
- **云村 ("Cloud Village")** — a named community layer wrapping the comment culture, connecting fans↔fans and fans↔creators.
- **Charts as fandom targets**: 飙升榜 (Soaring — 100 fastest-rising songs, recomputed daily), 新歌榜, 原创榜, 热歌榜. The daily recomputation makes the Soaring chart a fandom battleground: a coordinated one-day push visibly moves it.
- Fandom playbook: repeat streaming, mass-liking existing comments, posting fresh comments to inflate discussion volume — the chart consumes free actions, which fans industrialize.

### What drives DAILY return
(a) The **daily-refresh chart** gives a fandom a concrete "today's job"; (b) the comment wall is an **ambient emotional feed** that renews without the fandom doing anything — a reason to open the app that survives the absence of a campaign. The annual listening report (年度报告) adds a once-a-year identity payoff for continuous logging.

### What was banned
- **August 2021**: QQ Music limited digital albums/singles to **one purchase per account** and removed charts identified as causing conflict between fan clubs; NetEase announced comparable purchase limits.
- The underlying abuse: fandoms bought thousands of copies of the same digital album per person to top sales charts.
- Analyst caution worth internalizing: **capping purchases per account just pushes fandoms into registering more accounts.** Any cap must be enforced at identity level, not account level, or it becomes a tax on the poorest fans and a non-event for organized ones.

## 5. Owhat / Modian-style collective fan funding

### Core mechanics
- **站子 (fansites)** act as the fundraising vehicle: post a target, collect via a funding platform, publish a ledger.
- Money goes to **应援 (support) projects**: birthday LED billboards, subway/bus takeovers, airport banners, food trucks, charity donations in the idol's name, aircraft liveries at the top end.
- Also funded the *infrastructure* of ranking warfare: bulk phone numbers, VIP memberships, sponsored-product bulk buys.
- **Scale**: China's idol-generated economy projected at ~**100 billion yuan by end-2021**, up from 45 billion in 2018.
- **Speed as spectacle**: a Chinese fan group for BTS's Jimin raised **over 1 million yuan in three minutes** to wrap a Jeju Air jet. The countdown-and-thermometer dynamic is the mechanic — velocity itself is the flex.

### What drives DAILY return
Not much — it is **campaign-shaped**: burst → deadline → reveal. Retention value comes from the reveal (photos of the billboard) and the public ledger, not a daily loop. Treat as an episodic layer on top of a daily loop, never as the loop itself.

### What was banned — the most heavily destroyed category
- **CAC 清朗 campaign (launched 15 June 2021)** named inducing minors to contribute money to idols as one of five categories of "chaos," alongside doxing/abuse, wealth-flaunting, comment manipulation, and manufactured topics.
- **From August 2021, fan-funding apps were pulled from Chinese app stores**: 超级星饭团, 魔饭生, 桃叭 (Taoba). **Owhat** restricted minors' spending, then on 14 October 2021 indefinitely halted its 粉丝会 merchant service, froze withdrawals, and began refunds; by 18 October it was gone from app stores. At the time it hosted 5,000+ fansite accounts with monthly transaction volume above 2 million yuan. December 2021 reporting described it as having "跑路" (absconded) with fansites unable to withdraw funds.
- Weibo suspended the Jimin fan account for 60 days (Sept 2021), characterizing the fundraising as illegal.
- Investigations found participants included minors and students with no independent income, plus fund misappropriation — why the category was treated as financial harm, not fandom exuberance.
- Modian (摩点) as a general reward-crowdfunding platform is a different regulatory animal (goods-delivery model). *[Current operating status unverified.]*

## Red lines: features NOT to copy

Not merely "risky" — these are the specific things Chinese regulators named and killed. Several would also fail app-store review or gambling/minors rules in other markets.

1. **Any leaderboard that ranks *people* by fan-generated popularity** — the ban explicitly extends to "disguised" lists. Rank communities, works, or activity — not humans.
2. **Pay-to-vote or purchase-multiplied votes**, especially product-carried vote codes (the milk-cap pattern).
3. **Ranking that consumes purchases** — repeat digital-album buying, gift-value-as-rank, virtual-flower spending.
4. **Collective fundraising toward an idol/creator tribute**, particularly with public contribution ledgers and countdown thermometers. This got platforms delisted, not just fined.
5. **Any spend path reachable by minors**, and any mechanic that rewards wealth display.
6. **Multi-account stacking as the intended power-user path.** Per-account caps without identity binding actively *create* the burner-account economy.

## Ranked top 5 transferable engagement features

### 1. Consecutive-day check-in streak, with public "biggest fan" status awarded for streak length
**Why it works:** Highest-intensity daily-return mechanic in the Chinese fan stack, costing the user nothing but one tap. The power is loss asymmetry: a streak is an accumulating public identity claim that one missed day destroys. It makes *time* the scarce resource rather than money — which is why it survived the crackdown while the paid Star Power List was deleted.
**Evidence:** Weibo Super Topics award "biggest fan" in the 名人堂 by consecutive check-in days; the check-in-driven Super Topic system continued operating after 明星势力榜 was removed (What's on Weibo; Sixth Tone).
**Build safely:** streak → personal level and cosmetic status only; never purchasable, giftable, or restorable for money.

### 2. A daily-drip scarce currency spent on creators/works (bilibili coins + 一键三连)
**Why it works:** (a) Forced daily return — ~1 coin/day, max 2 per item, so supporting what you love mathematically requires showing up daily. (b) A costly signal that stays free — because coins are rationed by time, giving one means something without money. The triple-action bundle collapses three support behaviors into a single memeable gesture.
**Build safely:** never sell the currency. The moment it is purchasable it becomes a paid ranking input and inherits every red line above.

### 3. Community-vs-community leaderboards ranked on free activity, with fan-run moderation
**Why it works:** Gives fandoms the tribal competition they want while sidestepping the banned pattern — the ranked entity is a **community's activity**, not a person's popularity, and inputs are free actions. Also solves moderation economics: Weibo hands 3 hosts + 10 sub-hosts real curation power and pays them purely in status; they do the work.
**Build safely:** rank groups/fandoms. Publish the input formula. Weight breadth (unique participants) over depth (actions per participant) so it cannot be won by a handful of grinders or bots.

### 4. Timestamp/position-anchored ambient commentary (danmaku) + like-ranked confessional comments (NetEase hot comments)
**Why it works:** Both manufacture **the feeling of a crowd out of asynchronous traffic**. Danmaku makes every solo viewing feel co-watched; NetEase's hot-comment wall gives users a reason to open the app independent of any campaign — critical for the retention *floor* between campaigns.
**Build:** anchor comments to a position in the artifact (timestamp, lyric line, image region), rank a "hot" shelf by likes, surface it on the object's landing view.

### 5. Free-grind wearable badges displayed next to your name everywhere you speak
**Why it works:** Converts every comment into a status broadcast — the badge is re-earned as *visible* thousands of times rather than sitting on an unvisited profile. bilibili's fan medal is the cleanest implementation: per-creator, level-graded, worn inline.
**Build safely:** copy the free levels-1–20 half, not the paid 21–40 half. Paid badge levels convert a status system into a wealth display.

**Deliberately excluded:** collective fan-funding (Owhat pattern) — strongest emotional payoff, worst risk profile (app-store delisting, absconded funds), and essentially no daily loop. *(Note: `FEATURE_RESEARCH.md` F5 "milestone projects" is effort-based — hearts/votes — not monetary; that distinction is exactly what separates it from this red line.)*
